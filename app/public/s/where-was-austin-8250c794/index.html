Where was Austin?

Seven photographs from a cockpit, sent to a group chat with no caption and no location. Every one of them had the GPS stripped. Here is how far you can still get.

Central Virginia, flying the Piedmont along the Blue Ridge.

The sun does the work. One frame taken at 07:00:35 with the sun already above the horizon pins the aircraft to a north-south corridor between 77.3 and 81.5 degrees west. That corridor runs straight through Charlottesville, Lynchburg and Roanoke. Everything west of it is ruled out by geometry that no aeroplane in these photographs could beat.

The photos carry no location

All seven files are HEIC from an iPhone 15 running iOS 26.6. A full metadata read returns 180 fields per file and not one is a GPS tag, a city, or a place name. For comparison, a photo in the same chat from a different phone returned 211 fields, and the extra block was the GPS data. The absence is real. The camera was not recording position.

What the files do carry is an exact capture time and the phone’s UTC offset: -04:00. Eastern Daylight Time.

The sun is the witness

A timestamp plus an observed lighting condition is a position fix. Sunrise happens at a different moment at every longitude, so if you know when the sun came up where someone was, you know roughly how far east or west they were.

Three morning frames bracket it. At 06:43:48 the horizon is on fire but the solar disc has not cleared it. At 07:00:35 the sun is up and flaring in the lens. Sunrise happened between those two moments, at his position and his altitude.

What that excludes

Because he was airborne the horizon sits below level and the sun rises early. That correction is worth about 1.7 degrees at 8,000 feet, and it is the only thing that could push the answer westward. It is nowhere near enough.

A strut-braced piston single does not fly at 19,600 feet, let alone 39,000. Tennessee, Ohio and everything further west are out.

One limit is harder than the other

The western edge is solid. Cloud can hide a sun that has risen, but nothing can show you a sun that is still below the horizon, so the 07:00:35 frame is a floor that cannot be argued down. The eastern edge is softer, because it rests on the sun not being visible at 06:43:48, and a low cloud bank could have produced that. The sky in that frame is clean, which is why the edge is drawn where it is, but treat 77.3 as the weaker of the two numbers.

The band is a longitude constraint. Near the equinox sunrise time barely changes with latitude, so the north-south extent is genuinely open. Terrain and a central Virginia phone number are what narrow it, and both are softer than the arithmetic.

The day, frame by frame

He was airborne before first light and still flying twelve hours later. The evening frames show him working around heavy rain.

What this does not establish

  • Latitude. The solar method constrains east and west well and north and south badly. He could be anywhere along that corridor.
  • The airport. Nothing in the imagery shows a runway, a sign, or a tail number.
  • Whether he was flying or riding along. The framing suggests the left seat, but that is an inference.
  • That both legs are the same aircraft. The wing and cowl look consistent, which is suggestive and not proof.
  • The offset records the timezone the phone believed it was in. It agrees with the terrain and the area code, so it is almost certainly right, but on its own it is not proof of position.
