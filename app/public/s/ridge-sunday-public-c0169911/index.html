Ridge Sunday

A leaf-peeping day on Skyline Drive with a cooler, two boys, and no rush.

The big decision

Go in at Front Royal. Skip the Luray plan.

Both gates jam
Equally busy

The Park Service says Front Royal and Thornton Gap (Luray) are the busiest entrances and have the longest waits. Luray doesn't get you around the line.

Time from home
About 45 min saved

Front Royal is about 1 hour away. Thornton Gap takes 1½ to 2 hours, and the fastest way there goes through Front Royal anyway.

Bonus
31 extra miles of Drive

Starting at mile 0 adds the northern overlooks and Dickey Ridge, the best early stop for the boys, before the middle stretch.

The "speed pass": there isn't a true fast lane. The best you can do is buy the $30 vehicle pass on Recreation.gov tonight and screenshot the barcode. Rangers may open a lane for pre-paid passes, and the new automated gates scan barcodes. Neither is guaranteed. The park is cashless, so bring a physical card too.

The honest answer

How long will we sit in traffic?

Probably 10 to 20 minutes at the gate if you get there around 9:05, and maybe 30 to 45 extra minutes over the whole day. The schedule already allows for most of it. Nobody publishes real wait times, so this is my estimate from local guides and past news reports.

Front Royal gate wait, by arrival time

Local outfitters and cabin hosts say the gates back up mostly between 9 and 11 AM on October weekends, and that before 8 or after 1 is easy. One visitor report mentions waits up to 30 minutes. This is the third weekend of October, one of the two busiest of the year.

Read the line yourself

At peak, the gate moves about 8 or 9 cars a minute (2018 news report). Roughly, every quarter mile of cars ahead of you is about 7 or 8 minutes.

On the Drive

Expect a slow parade at 25 to 35 mph and full overlook lots in the middle of the day. The schedule allows about twice the posted-speed drive time between stops.

Going home

There's no line to leave at Thornton Gap. Plan 10 to 20 extra minutes for leaf traffic through Sperryville and Front Royal on a Sunday afternoon.

If you can nudge the start

Leaving at 7:30 instead of 8:00 puts you at the gate around 8:35, before the 9 o'clock build-up. That likely saves 10 to 15 minutes.

The schedule

Leave at 8, home by 5

    For James and Jude

    Earning the Junior Ranger badges

    The badge is free, and it's an official park badge sworn in by a ranger. The booklet is aimed at ages 7 to 12, and an adult has to help. Each boy needs his own booklet. Here's exactly what it takes, and how it fits your day.

    1

    Get the booklets at Dickey Ridge (mile 4.6)

    They're free at the front desk, which opens at 9 on Sunday. Ask for one per boy, and ask whether a ranger program is on today. The schedule is only posted at the visitor centers, and none is listed online for this weekend.

    2

    Do "Arrowhead Adventures" on page one

    Everyone has to do it, and it doesn't count toward the five pages. It's about the National Park Service arrowhead, the logo on the entrance signs and on every ranger's patch. Point it out at the gate.

    3

    Finish five more pages of their choice

    The Fox Hollow Trail TRACK guide counts as one page. Pick up the brochure at the trailhead across the road from the visitor center and do it on the 1.2-mile loop. That leaves four pages for the car ride and the picnic. (Limberlost at mile 43 has a TRACK guide too, if you want a backup.)

    4

    Attend a ranger program, or watch one online

    If a ranger program is on, go and get the ranger's signature in the booklet. If there isn't one, the park allows a Virtual Junior Ranger video instead. Watch it tonight, since there's no signal in the park. The shortest is Ranger Anna's black bears video (9:40). Ranger Elsie's arrowhead video (17:20) also helps with page one. Write down the video's name and one thing they learned in the booklet.

    5

    Have a ranger review the booklet, then recite the pledge

    The ranger checks the booklet, the boys raise their right hands and say the Junior Ranger pledge, and they get their badges. This can happen at Dickey Ridge or Byrd Visitor Center at Big Meadows (mile 51), both open until 5 PM on Sunday.

    The catch: where to get them sworn in

    Full day

    Easiest. You arrive at Byrd Visitor Center around 3:15 with the booklets done. Get sworn in, then run the meadow.

    Classic + Byrd

    After Stony Man, drive 9 more miles south to Byrd (about 20 minutes), get the badges, then head back north to Thornton Gap. That adds about 45 minutes, so you'd be home around 5:45.

    Mail it in

    Finish at home and mail the booklets to: Shenandoah National Park, ATTN: Junior Ranger Program, 3655 US Highway 211 East, Luray, VA 22835. Include your return address. They'll miss the in-person pledge, though.

    Dickey Ridge comes too early in the day. You pass it at 9:25, before the booklets are done.

    For Ash

    Little presents along the way

    Small surprises tied to the moments that need a boost: the start, a finished hike, a summit, and the long ride home. They're tagged present in the schedule above.

    At the Front Royal gate

    Something to use all day: kid binoculars, a little compass, or a nature bingo card for spotting deer, hawks and red leaves.

    After the Fox Hollow loop

    A reward for the first hike, good alongside the Junior Ranger booklets: a magnifying glass, a field journal, or colored pencils.

    On top of Stony Man

    The summit prize, small enough for a pocket. Hand it out after everyone is back from the cliff edge.

    The drive home

    Something quiet for 1½ hours in the car: a sticker book, a small puzzle, or a park animal plush.

    Buying them in the park instead? Dickey Ridge and Byrd visitor centers have park shops (open 9 to 5 on Sunday), and the Elkwallow and Skyland waysides sell souvenirs (open through Nov 8). Patches, stuffed animals, and Junior Ranger gear are the usual finds.

    Good to know

    Ridge rules for a family day

    Full lot? Keep driving.

    There are about 75 overlooks. If one is packed, the next one is usually a few minutes away. Park with all four wheels off the pavement or you may be towed.

    Gas before the gate

    Fill up in Charles Town or Berryville. The only pumps in the park are at Big Meadows (mile 51).

    No signal most of the way

    Download offline maps tonight. Dickey Ridge has cell service, and Byrd Visitor Center at Big Meadows has free Wi-Fi.

    Junior Ranger badges

    Full step-by-step plan in the Junior Ranger section above. Watch a ranger video tonight.

    Skip these with little legs

    Dark Hollow Falls is steep and crowded on the climb back up. Marys Rock is 3 to 3.5 hours round trip. Old Rag needs a separate ticket.
