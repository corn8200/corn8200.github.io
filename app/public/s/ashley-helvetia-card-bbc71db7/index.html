Hurricane to Helvetia

Monday 27 July 2026 · screenshot this before you leave

Read this part twice

There is no cell service in Helvetia. Not weak service. None. It fades out somewhere in the last 20 miles of backroad and does not come back until you leave the valley.

911 will not reach anyone by cell from the village. Your options are the inn wifi, the library, or satellite. All three are further down.

Screenshot every section now, while you still have signal.

What time to leave

8:00 AM

The drive is about 2 hours 40 minutes. Leaving at 8:00 gets you to Helvetia around 10:40, in time for the 11:00 walking tour with John and the boys.

It also puts you behind most of the morning fog. There is patchy fog near Hurricane and Charleston when you set out, thinning as you go, and gone by the time you are past Sutton.

7:00 is the one hour to avoid. It is the thickest of the fog and it buys you nothing but sitting in Helvetia waiting for the tour to start.

Driving it, if you go early

  • Low beams only. High beams bounce off the fog and make it worse. Fog lights on if you have them.
  • Use the white line on your right as your guide, not the center line. Following the center line pulls you toward oncoming traffic.
  • Double your following distance. Most of this is interstate and the real risk is the car ahead stopping suddenly.
  • If it gets bad enough to stop, get off at an exit. Never stop on the shoulder. In fog, people drive into parked cars because they follow taillights.
  • Crack a window and listen. You will hear a truck before you see one.
  • Give yourself margin. If it slows you down, you are just later, and that is fine.

Your phone will lie to you

Never follow anything labeled "WV 46." That is a different road on the far side of the state, about 110 road miles away. You want County Route 46, which the signs call Adolph Road.

Refuse these three, worst first:

  1. "Chemical & Helvetia Railroad." A real county route numbered CR 46/7, so it looks like a version of the road you want. It is 4.6 miles of solid gravel and it branches off the correct road less than two miles from the village. This is the one most likely to catch you.
  2. Carter Road. The gravel cutoff off Alexander Road.
  3. Old Helvetia Road. Branches off the paved road a hundred feet from where you are going, with a name that sounds right.

When the road signs and the phone disagree, the signs win. The village says so itself: GPS is not reliable there.

Where to wait

Helvetia Public Library, 4901 Pickens Rd. Open Monday 10:00 to 6:00. Wifi, computers, a bathroom and a roof. It is the one place reliably open on a Monday.

The post office, inside the Swiss Roots building, is open 10:00 to 2:00 on weekdays. The Hutte restaurant is closed Sunday through Wednesday, so do not plan on walking there for a meal or a phone.

Five minutes at home, before you go

Your iPhone can text 911 through a satellite with no bars at all. It is text only, and it needs an iPhone 14 or newer.

  1. Update it. Settings, General, Software Update.
  2. Fill in Medical ID. Health app, tap your picture, tap Medical ID. Add John as an emergency contact. Responders get this automatically and John is told where you are.
  3. Share your location with John in Find My. Off the grid, your phone can still push a location by satellite.
  4. Practice once. Outside with plenty of sky: swipe down from the top right, tap Cellular, tap Satellite, then Try Demo. It does not call 911.

That practice run is worth more than everything else here. If the demo will not run, tell John before you leave.

If you need help and have no bars

  1. Dial 911 anyway. Sometimes a call goes through when nothing else will.
  2. If it will not connect, the screen offers Emergency Text via Satellite. Tap it, then tap Report Emergency.
  3. Answer by tapping. The questions are short and multiple choice.
  4. Get out of the car, into the open. It will not connect from inside a car or a building. Check for traffic first. If you are hurt and cannot get out, still try.
  5. Get away from heavy cover. Thin branches only slow it. What blocks it is dense leaves, cliffs and steep valley walls. Helvetia is in a valley, so terrain is the real problem. Walk toward open ground.
  6. Hold the phone normally, low in your hand. Do not raise it toward the sky. Do not put it in a pocket.
  7. The screen tells you which way to turn. Turn slowly when it asks. If it says move, move.
  8. Wait longer than feels right. Thirty seconds under open sky, over a minute under trees, sometimes a few minutes. Do not quit and start over.

If you start a session by accident, do not close it. Wait for a person and tell them you are fine.

Car trouble, and texting John

Flat tire, locked out, out of gas. Open Messages, type Roadside in the To field, tap Roadside Assistance. Same outdoor steps as above.

To text John when you are safe but out of service, send it normally. When it fails, the phone offers satellite.

Photos, videos and group texts will not go by satellite, only plain text. And that is not the emergency path. In real trouble use 911.

Before you get there

  • Arrive with a full tank. Fill at Flatwoods, I-79 Exit 67. Right at the ramp.
  • Treat the whole area as having no fuel. There is none in the village. The line about a station in Pickens traces to website wording unchanged since 2021, and that store changed hands in January. Nearest fuel anyone can vouch for is Buckhannon, about an hour back.
  • Bring cash. No ATM in the village.
  • The Hutte is closed Sunday through Wednesday. John is buying groceries in Elkins and cooking, so there is no food to find in town.
  • Last week's flooding was severe here. If you come on water across the road, turn around. Do not guess at the depth.

Driving home Tuesday

You leave at 8:00 Tuesday morning, sharp. Severe storms arrive Tuesday afternoon and the point is to be home ahead of them. Load the car Monday night.

You will be in two cars. Stay behind John and let him lead. For the first 40 minutes out of the valley neither phone works.

If you get separated, pull over in the next town with signal and wait. Do not keep driving to find each other. Agree on that town before you pull out.
