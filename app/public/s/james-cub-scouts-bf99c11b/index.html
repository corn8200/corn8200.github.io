Getting James into Cub Scouts

He starts as a Lion, the kindergarten rank. One of us goes to every meeting with him. Here is the pack, the people to contact, the real cost, and what to do before the fourteenth.

Monday, September 14 · 6:30 to 7:30 PM

Pack 420, South Jefferson Elementary School, 4599 Summit Point Rd, Charles Town. About 24 minutes from the house.

Of every pack within reach, Pack 420 is the only one that meets on a Monday night, weekly, close to home. Two separate sources agree on the night and the address: Scouting America's live registration system and the pack's own website. It has 21 registered kids and has been chartered for 29 years.

Do not just drive out there. Their website has not been updated since 2024 and their events page is empty, so nothing publicly confirms a meeting happens on the fourteenth. Their program year "begins in September" with no date named. Send the email first.

What to do this week

In order. The first one is the whole ballgame.

  1. Email Pack 420 and ask three things. Is the first meeting Monday September 14 at 6:30. Can we bring a kindergartner to visit before registering. Is there a Lion den running this year. Write to CubScoutPack420.SJES@gmail.com.
  2. If nobody answers in two days, call the council. Shenandoah Area Council, (540) 662-2551. They can tell you whether a unit is actually meeting, which is exactly what a stale website cannot. Jefferson County is their Potomac District.
  3. Decide which of us is the adult partner. A Lion cannot be dropped off. Whoever takes him is staying for the whole hour, every time.
  4. Do not buy a uniform yet. Not until we have seen a meeting and know the den is real. The list of what he actually needs is below, and it is not what the Scout Shop bundle sells you.
  5. Register only after the visit. Nothing requires paying before you visit. The direct application link for this pack is on BeAScout.

What to buy and what to skip

The Scout Shop sells a "Lion Kindergarten Uniform Bundle" for $101.93. It is built around next year's shirt.

Actually required

  • Lion t-shirt, navy, $14.99
  • Cub Scout web belt, $14.99
  • Lion cap, $22.99

Skip for now

  • The blue button-up shirt. That starts at Tiger, in first grade
  • Uniform pants and socks, optional
  • Neckerchief and slide, the den decides

The belt matters more than it looks. He earns a belt loop for each adventure he completes, and they go on that belt. There is no uniform inspection for Lions, so nobody is going to check him.

The adult partner rule

This is the part that changes our calendar, so it is worth being exact about.

A Lion has to have an adult partner with him at every meeting and every activity. Pack 420 states it on their own site: the adult partner has to accompany and remain with the scout at all scouting events. This is not a drop-off program for another year, until he moves up to Wolf.

What that does not require

  • A parent partner does not have to register as a leader. The position was taken off the roster in 2023 and is now just a checkbox on his application.
  • Safeguarding Youth Training is highly recommended, not required, for a parent partner. It is free, online, about 90 minutes, and worth doing anyway.
  • No background check and no adult application, as long as the partner lives at the same address he does.

Where that flips

If we ever send him with an adult who does not live here, that person needs a full adult application, the training, and a background check that asks for a Social Security number and ten years of addresses. Worth knowing before we ask a grandparent to cover a night.

Also worth knowing: a Lion adult partner does not count toward the two-adult minimum a den needs. So our being there does not relieve the pack of finding a second registered leader.

The honest risk

What could make the fourteenth a wasted trip.

Pack 420's registration is genuinely current. Someone checked it live on September 2 and the online application flow is open and accepting kindergartners today. That part is solid.

Everything the pack publishes about itself is not. The website has not been meaningfully edited since April 2025. The calendar link points at May 2024. The About page has the wrong pack number on it. The only announcement is about a crossover ceremony, which is end-of-year language. Their Facebook page last posted publicly in September 2023.

And the specific thing that matters most for James: on their own leaders page, the Lion den leader is listed as TBD. Nobody is named. That page is stale enough that it may have been filled since, but the only published evidence says the kindergarten den has no leader. If that is still true, the practical answer may be that a parent has to run it.

Two things worth knowing

Jude cannot join yet. Cub Scouts starts at kindergarten and there is no pre-K rank. Lion is the only one of the six ranks with no age-based alternative, so turning four in November does not help. His year is fall 2028.

Merit badges and Eagle are a long way off. Those belong to Scouts BSA, which starts around age eleven. Cub Scouts has none of that. James works on adventures instead: six required and two electives to earn the Lion badge, starting with Bobcat. Then Tiger, Wolf, Bear, Webelos, and Arrow of Light, one per school year.

One of the Lion requirements is homework for us rather than him. Bobcat asks the parent to go through a booklet on protecting children from abuse, together, at home.
